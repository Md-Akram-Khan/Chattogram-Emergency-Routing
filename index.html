      Real-World Dynamic Cost & Heuristic Model for Chattogram Metro

      Every node can be chosen as a start or destination from the search boxes, from a named place, or by clicking the map. The road network is built from OpenStreetMap data for the Chattogram metropolitan area, from motorways down to residential streets and public service lanes (old-city galis, market and campus lanes), including Kalurghat and the south bank: every junction keeps its true OSM position, pass-through points are folded into curved road segments that follow the real road line, and one-way directions are kept. The graph is the largest strongly connected part of the network, so every node can reach every other node without driving against a one-way street. The 47 curated landmarks (port gates, CEPZ, depots, circles, flyovers, hospitals and other well-known places) are snapped onto the road they lie on. Flood susceptibility and night safety for each OSM road are interpolated from the hand-calibrated seed corridors.

      1. Dynamic Edge Cost Function C(u, v)

      The dynamic traversal cost for an emergency vehicle along road segment (u, v) is formulated as:

      C(u, v) = Distance × w_road × (1 + Wt·Traffic) × (1 + Wr·Water) × (1 + Wc·Condition) × (1 + Ws·SafetyPenalty) + JunctionDelay

      Wt, Wr, Wc, Ws are the adjustable cost weights (default 1). Closed directions, and the wrong way along one-way streets, are excluded from the graph instead of being given a finite penalty.

      2. Condition Precedence

      Conditions for each road are resolved in this order, later entries replacing earlier ones: (1) the baseline sliders; (2) the active scenario's conditions for the road's OSM class; (3) the active scenario's conditions for that specific road; (4) the saved user override for that road. A saved override can therefore reopen a road that a scenario closes. Switching back to Normal Day does not remove saved overrides; use "Reset All Road Overrides" for that.

      3. Admissible Heuristic Function H(n)

      For informed search algorithms (A* Search and Greedy BFS), the heuristic scales straight-line Haversine distance by the cheapest cost per straight-line km of any passable road direction under the current conditions:

      H(n, Goal) = HaversineDistance(n, Goal) × min(u,v) [ C(u, v) / HaversineDistance(u, v) ]

      Because every road costs at least that much per km of straight-line progress, H never overestimates (admissible) and never drops by more than an edge's cost (consistent), so A* returns the same optimal route as UCS. The "A* with zero heuristic" baseline runs the same engine with H = 0, which shows how much work the heuristic saves.

      4. Search Limits and Result Status

      Each search stops at the configured maximum expansions, timeout, or (for DFS and IDS) maximum depth, and reports expansion_limit, timeout or depth_limit. Only unreachable, reported after a search has explored everything it can reach, means that no route exists.

      5. Estimated Travel Time

      Drive time uses free-flow speeds per road type (arterial 50, avenue 40, street 28, alley 15, broken 12 km/h), slowed by the same traffic, waterlogging and road-condition multipliers, plus junction waits. Night safety changes which route is preferred, not how fast the vehicle drives. All traffic, weather, condition and safety values are simulations; no live data feed is connected.
